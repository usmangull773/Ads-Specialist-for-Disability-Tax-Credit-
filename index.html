02 / YOUR CALL

Pay per click or pay per lead. Here is the trade, plainly.

You asked to understand the trade-off before committing, so this is the honest version of both, and what each one does to the data your SEO side depends on.

Pay per click

You own the account and the data

What works for you

  • The account, the history, and the conversion data stay yours.
  • You see every search term that spent money, which is exactly what the SEO side needs.
  • Cost per lead comes down as the negatives and bids tighten week to week.

What it asks of you

  • Tracking has to be set and verified first, so setup is more careful.
  • The first few weeks are a learning phase, not full speed.
  • It needs active management, not set and forget.

Pay per lead

A third party owns the data

What looks easy

  • A fixed price per lead feels predictable on paper.
  • Less setup exposure at the very start.

What you give up

  • You do not see the search terms, so the SEO feedback loop goes dark.
  • Lead quality and verification are harder to control or dispute.
  • You are renting results instead of building an asset you keep.
Recommendation

Start on pay per click. For a credibility-sensitive service where your SEO consultant needs the search-term data, keeping the account, the tracking, and the term-level insight in your own hands is worth the extra care up front. Pay per lead can stay on the table as a test later, once there is real data to compare it against.

03 / FIRST 30 DAYS

How the first month actually runs.

No mystery, no vague ramp-up. Each stage has a job, and the order is deliberate.

Day 0

Set the tracking, then prove it

GA4, the Google Ads conversion, and the eligibility-form event on the landing page. Nothing goes live until a test submission shows up correctly.

Days 1–3

Launch tight, not wide

DTC-only ad groups grouped by applicant intent, a day-one negative list, and ad copy that stays inside Google's rules for this category.

Days 4–14

Read the search terms daily

Add negatives as researcher phrases appear, pause what is not converting, and move budget toward the ad groups that bring real enquiries.

Days 15–30

Tune toward cost per qualified lead

Bids and budget shift to the terms that produce genuine applicants, and the first monthly view lands: spend, clicks, conversions, cost per qualified lead.

Monthly

Report, then align with SEO

Spend, clicks, conversions, and cost per qualified lead, plus a short call with your SEO consultant on which terms turned into leads.

Day 0 is the gate. GA4, the Google Ads conversion, and the landing-page event all fire on a real test lead before a single dollar goes live.
04 / SAFEGUARDS

Built for a sensitive category, and to feed your SEO side.

Disability sits under Google's sensitive-category rules, so targeting and claims both matter. Run it the wrong way and the account risks disapprovals or suspension. This is where a plan for a regulated service differs from a generic one.

How this campaign runs

  • Targets what people search, not who they are.
  • Compliant copy: eligibility help, not guaranteed outcomes.
  • Location and intent do the work, within policy.

What it never does

  • No targeting people by health or disability.
  • No promised refund amounts or approval guarantees.
  • Nothing that risks the ad account.
